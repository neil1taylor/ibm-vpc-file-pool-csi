A pool filling up

A pool is a group of shares. Each volume takes a number of GB on one share. When the shares fill up, the pool can add a new share by itself. This page shows when that happens, when a warning alert fires, and what the other percent numbers mean. The sizes here are examples: each share is 100 GB, and each square is 10 GB.

Which percent is which

Two different measures are easy to mix up. Allocated is the GB the driver has promised to volumes (and snapshots). Written is the bytes really stored on the share. A 50 GB volume that holds 2 GB is 50 GB allocated and 2 GB written.
