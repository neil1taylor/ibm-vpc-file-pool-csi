# Spec: survive the loss of a zone (multi-zone clusters)

Date: 2026-10-22. Status: draft for review. Nothing in this spec is built yet.

## Goal

A ROVS or ROKS cluster has workers in three zones of one region. One zone fails. Then:

- VMs and pods that used pool storage in that zone start again in another zone, with their data.
- We state in advance how much recent data can be lost (the **recovery point**, RPO) and how long the restart takes (the **recovery time**, RTO). The console and the metrics show the real numbers at any moment.
- Two copies of one VM never write to the same disk at the same time (**fencing**).
- When the zone returns, the workloads can move back (**failback**), or can stay where they are.

"Done" means a lab test with a simulated zone loss passes for both protection levels below, and an admin can run the whole failover and failback from the console or one command.

What this spec does not promise: a VM does not keep running through a zone loss. Its memory is lost. It starts again from its disk, like a power cut. No IBM Cloud feature can change that for a VM whose host is gone.

## What exists today

All facts in this section come from the code and the docs in this repository.

- A pool lives in one zone (`FileSharePool.spec.zone`). Its shares and their mount targets are created in that zone.
- A multi-zone cluster uses one pool per zone and a `WaitForFirstConsumer` StorageClass. The driver reads the zone of the node that the pod was scheduled to (`topology.kubernetes.io/zone`) and picks the pool of that zone. The example is in `examples/multi-zone/`.
- A pool can list `accessorZones`. The driver then gives the volume a topology that allows every listed zone. `VPC-NETWORKING.md` says one mount target in the home zone serves all zones. `KNOWN-LIMITATIONS.md` ("Cross-Zone Node Failover") says there is one mount target per zone. The two pages disagree. This project has not tested cross-zone mounting on a multi-zone cluster. Phase 0 settles it.
- Pools only use the `dp2` profile (zonal). The VPC client sends the profile name from the pool and has no regional-share code.
- Replication (`ReplicationPolicy`) copies a pool to a destination: an NFS server or a receiver. It is built for another cluster or region. For running VMs it takes a QEMU copy of all the VM's disks at one moment, sends only changed blocks, and keeps 24 hours of restore points at the destination. `kubectl failover` then creates the SubVolume, PersistentVolume (PV) and PersistentVolumeClaim (PVC) at the destination and picks the newest moment that all disks of a VM can show.
- Nothing protects against the loss of a zone. If a zone fails, every volume in its pool is unavailable until the zone returns. A VM cannot start in another zone with its data.

### What this means

- **No synchronous copy exists for `dp2`.** IBM offers one synchronous design only: the regional `rfs` share. For `dp2`, the best IBM gives is asynchronous replication at 15 minutes or more. That is the size of the gap: **RPO 0 with `rfs`, and minutes with `dp2`. There is no `dp2` option with RPO 0.**
- `rfs` is not general availability. We cannot promise it to every customer. It also has real limits: a fixed 35,000 IOPS per share, higher write latency, and a 1 TB largest file. A VM disk is one file (`disk.img`), so a disk larger than 1 TB cannot live on `rfs`.

## The design that meets the goal

Two protection levels, chosen per pool. Both end in the same failover command and the same console page.

### Level 1: regional pool (RPO 0)

A pool can be regional. Its shares use the `rfs` profile. IBM keeps the data in all three zones. The pool has no home zone and no copies to manage.

When a zone fails, the shares stay up (fact 8, to verify in the lab). Nothing needs to move. The work is to fence the dead zone and restart the VMs in the other zones.

- RPO: **0**. The data is crash consistent, as after a power cut.
- RTO: fence and restart. Target under 10 minutes after the admin confirms the loss. Measure it in phase 0.
- Failback: nothing to copy back. When the zone returns, the admin lifts the fence. VMs can live-migrate to any zone, because the storage is the same everywhere.
- Needs: IBM special access for `rfs` in the region, and a lab check that the driver can create and mount an `rfs` share.
- Limits: facts 7 and 9. The pool webhook refuses a regional pool with a StorageClass that asks for more than 35,000 IOPS per share. The console warns about disks above 1 TB.

### Level 2: copied pool (RPO of minutes)

Each zone keeps its `dp2` pool, as today. Each pool is **protected by** a pool in another zone. The existing replication copies the pool's volumes to the partner pool, on a schedule, in the same cluster.

- VMs use the existing VM-safe copy. One QEMU step copies all disks of a VM at one moment. Multi-disk VMs stay consistent. Only changed blocks move. The destination keeps 24 hours of restore points.
- Plain volumes use the existing file copy.
- Partner layout: a ring. Zone 1 is copied to zone 2, zone 2 to zone 3, zone 3 to zone 1. Copies go into a separate folder tree on the partner pool's shares (`/replica/<source pool>/`). They do not appear as volumes and cannot be mounted by a pod.
- RPO: the time since the last good copy. The copy interval is 5 minutes by default. A copy with little change takes tens of seconds, so the RPO is **about 5 to 10 minutes**. A VM that writes fast or has large changes can take longer, and its RPO grows. The status shows the real value for each VM, and an alert fires when it passes the target.
- RTO: the failover command re-points each volume to its copy (below). Target 15 to 20 minutes for 50 VMs. Measure it in phase 0.
- Failback: planned, one VM at a time, with a short outage (below).
- Cost: the partner pool needs room for the copies and the restore points. See "Cost".

### Why not IBM share replication for Level 2

IBM share replication is not our recommended method. It does **not** meet the goal for VMs, for these reasons.

- The shortest interval is 15 minutes, and a run takes time on top. The practical RPO is 30 minutes or more, against 5 to 10 minutes for our copy.
- A share holds many volumes. IBM replicates the whole share, with all its capacity, whether the volumes need it or not.
- Failover splits the pair for good. After the zone returns, you create a new replica and send the whole share again.
- Failover is one share at a time, with no view of VMs. A VM with disks on two shares has no common moment. Our copy gives one moment for all disks.
- The docs do not say whether a sync gives a consistent moment (fact 15). If it does not, a running VM's `disk.img` can be torn, which is the same fault that made us stop using rsync for VM disks.

IBM replication stays on the list as a **comparison in phase 0**. If the lab shows that it gives a crash-consistent moment for each share, it is a possible lower-effort method for plain volumes. It is not part of the first build.

### Options that do not meet the goal

| Option | Why it does not meet the goal |
|---|---|
| Do nothing; restore from backup after the zone returns | Volumes stay down until the zone returns. No restart in another zone. |
| Restore from backup (COS) into another zone | Works, but the RPO is the backup interval (hours) and the RTO is hours for large disks. Keep it as the last resort. |
| IBM share replication alone | Meets "restart in another zone" with a 30 minute or worse RPO and no VM-safe moment. See above. |
| Cross-zone mounting alone (accessor zones) | Lets pods in zone 2 use a share from zone 1. It does not help when zone 1 fails, because the share is in zone 1. It is useful for load, not for survival. |
| Application-level replication (database clusters, for example) | Good, and outside this driver. We do not stop anyone from using it. |

## Failure model

| Failure | What happens | What the design does |
|---|---|---|
| Zone down (workers and storage in it are gone) | Pods in the zone are gone. Zonal shares in it are unreachable. | The goal of this spec. Failover moves the workloads. |
| Zone down, regional pool | Pods in the zone are gone. The `rfs` share stays up (to verify). | Fence the zone, restart VMs. No data moves. |
| Zone partition (zone is alive but cut off from the control plane or other zones) | Workers in the zone keep running their VMs and keep writing to their disks. The rest of the cluster thinks they are dead. **This is the dangerous case.** | Fencing (below). Without it, two VMs can write to one `rfs` disk and destroy it. |
| One share unavailable (zone is up) | Volumes on that share fail. The pool already marks a `failed` or `degraded` share and drains it. | Out of scope here. Level 2 copies let an admin fail over a pool, so the zone failover command also works for one pool (`--pool`). |
| Storage up, workers gone | Same as zone down, for the workers only. | Level 1: fence and restart. Level 2: the primary share is up, so volumes could stay. We still use the zone failover, because the VM needs workers with its data, and the data is only mountable from the zone's workers if cross-zone mounting works. Phase 0 checks cross-zone mounting from a surviving zone. If it works, a Level 2 volume can be mounted across zones with no data movement ("storage-only-up" shortcut). |
| Control plane unavailable | OpenShift on ROKS runs its control plane across zones. This spec assumes it survives one zone. | Not covered. |

## Placement

- **Pools per zone** stay the same. The multi-zone StorageClass with `WaitForFirstConsumer` places a VM's disk in the VM's zone, so reads and writes stay in-zone.
- **A regional pool** has a StorageClass with `WaitForFirstConsumer`, but any zone is allowed. The scheduler places the VM on any node.
- **VM-to-zone affinity.** A VM keeps its zone while that zone is healthy. We do not add a new rule. After a failover, the VM runs in the partner zone.
- **Spare room.** To survive one zone of three, the other two zones must hold the load of the third. Each zone can run at most 66% of its capacity. The console shows the headroom and refuses a failover plan when the target zone lacks CPU, memory, or pool space (admin may override).
- **No partner by accident.** The pool webhook refuses a partner in the same zone as the source, and refuses two pools that protect each other when a third zone exists (use the ring).

## New objects

- `FileSharePool.spec.zoneProtection` (Level 2 only): `partnerPool` (a pool in another zone), `interval` (default 5m), `rpoTarget` (default 15m), `keepRestorePoints` (default 24h). The controller creates and owns a `ReplicationPolicy` for it. The admin does not write the policy.
- `FileSharePool.spec.profile: rfs` (Level 1). The zone is optional for a regional pool.
- `ZoneFailover` (cluster-wide): `failedZone`, `mode` (`Failover` or `Failback`), `pools` (default all in the zone), `rewindTo` (optional), `dryRun`, `approvedBy`. Its status lists each step with a result, so the record shows what was done and when. One `ZoneFailover` per zone at a time.
- `FileSharePool.status.zoneState`: `Healthy`, `Suspect`, `Failed`, `FailedOver`, `Recovering`.

## Detecting a zone loss

A new controller watches three signals for each zone:

1. Node health: the share of workers in the zone that are `NotReady`.
2. Storage health: the share and mount target state from the VPC API, and a small probe Job in a surviving zone that lists a folder on the share.
3. Node agent heartbeat: each node agent already runs on every worker. A missing heartbeat from the whole zone counts as a signal.

States:

- **Suspect**: signal 1 or 3 is true for 2 minutes. An alert fires. Nothing else changes.
- **Failed (proposed)**: two of three signals are true for 5 minutes. The console shows "Zone X looks down. Fail over?" with the plan. This is the point where a human decides.

While a zone is `Suspect` or `Failed`, the pool manager in that zone's pools stops auto-expand and stops creating shares. It never creates a replacement share in a failed zone. It keeps every SubVolume record, so no data is forgotten.

The VPC API for a failed zone can be slow or can fail. Every VPC call in this path has a time limit and is allowed to fail without blocking the rest of the plan.

## Failover

### Who decides: an admin, not the controller (first release)

Failover is **admin-triggered** (console button or `kubectl failover zone execute`). The controller proposes it. It does not run it alone.

Why: a wrong failover is worse than a slow one. A false alarm (a network blip) with an automatic failover would start a second copy of every VM and could corrupt `rfs` disks. A zone loss is rare, and a few minutes for a human to confirm costs little against that risk.

An automatic mode is a later phase (phase 6). It is off by default and requires all three signals, a longer wait (15 minutes), and a limit of one automatic failover per day.

### Fencing

Fencing means making sure the old copy of a VM cannot write any more. It happens **before** any VM restarts. The command refuses to continue until every fence step is done or the admin says in writing (`--accept-unfenced`) that the zone is physically gone.

Fence steps, in order:

1. **Storage network.** Remove the failed zone's worker subnets from the security group rules of the mount targets of the affected shares. This cuts NFS traffic from that zone at the VPC. It works from a surviving zone, because the VPC API is regional. Lab check: does removing the rule stop traffic on connections that are already open? (Security groups track connections, so it may not.)
2. **Workers.** Stop the failed zone's worker instances through the VPC API, if the API answers. Otherwise delete the Node objects.
3. **Nodes in Kubernetes.** Delete the Node objects of the zone. Red Hat documents this as the trigger to restart `Always` VMs elsewhere (fact 16). Force-delete the stuck `virt-launcher` pods.
4. **Taint.** Taint the zone `storage.ibmcloud.io/zone-failed:NoExecute` so nothing schedules there. The taint is removed at failback.
5. **File lock.** The NFS server drops the locks of a client that does not renew its lease. QEMU also takes a lock on its image file. If a zombie client later reconnects, it should find its state expired. Lab check: do its writes fail? This is defence in depth only. We do not rely on it.

For Level 2 the two copies are separate files, so a zombie VM in the dead zone cannot damage the copy. The risk is **two diverging versions** of a VM, not corruption. The failback step (below) picks the winner and the other version is kept, not deleted, until the admin removes it.

### Steps: regional pool (Level 1)

1. Plan: list the VMs and pods in the zone, the spare room in the other zones, and the state of the pool.
2. Fence (above).
3. Restart: VMs with `Always` or `RerunOnFailure` restart by themselves after step 2. The command waits and reports each VM. VMs with `Halted` stay off. Plain pods that belong to a Deployment or StatefulSet restart as usual.
4. Report: the time from approval to the last VM running, and which VMs did not start.

### Steps: copied pool (Level 2)

1. Plan: for each volume in the failed pool, the moment of its last good copy (the RPO), the VM group, and the spare room. VMs whose disks show different moments get the newest common moment, as in the existing failover.
2. Fence (above).
3. Stop new copies from the failed zone. Run the existing recovery step on each copy: roll back any half-applied copy.
4. **Re-home** each volume, in the same cluster:
   1. Delete the old PVC and PV objects (the data in the failed zone is not touched; the PV has `Retain`).
   2. Move the SubVolume record to the partner pool, to the folder of the copy.
   3. Create a new PV that points to the partner pool's share and the copy's folder, with a node affinity for the partner zone. Create the PVC again with the same name and namespace, bound to that PV.
   4. The VM object already exists in the cluster, so it restarts on its own PVC names. Nothing in the VM manifest changes.
5. Move the copy folder out of `/replica/` into `/pvcs/` on the same share. This is a rename inside one share, so it is instant.
6. Start the VMs (they start after step 2 by themselves), and wait for each.
7. Report the RPO per VM and the moment each VM runs from.

A rewind (`rewindTo`) uses the existing rewind: a new disk is built from the latest disk and the undo files. It is slower and needs room for one extra disk.

Open point for phase 0: deleting and recreating a PVC that a pod used, on a node that is gone. The `pvc-protection` finalizer waits for the pod to end. Step 2 must have removed the pod first. The test must show that it works with a stuck pod.

### Volumes made or changed during the outage

- New PVCs go to the pools of the surviving zones. A PVC of a failed pool that is still `Pending` stays pending. The event says why.
- `DeleteVolume` for a volume in a failed pool answers with a retriable error. It does not forget the volume.
- Snapshots and backups of volumes in a failed pool are paused. The backup history is kept by volume name, so it continues after the re-home (to verify in phase 0: the backup policy selects by PVC and labels, not by pool).

## Failback

### Regional pool

Lift the fence: remove the taint, uncordon the nodes (or let the replaced workers join). VMs may stay where they are, or live-migrate back one by one. No data moves.

### Copied pool

The failed pool in the returned zone holds old data. It is now **stale**: the VMs ran on the partner pool since the failover. The driver keeps it read-only and out of use.

1. **Re-seed.** The zone's pool is reconnected. A reverse protection starts: partner pool to the returned pool. The first run is a full copy with the compare pass, so only blocks that differ cross the network. The status shows the progress and the RPO.
2. **Choose when.** The admin chooses a quiet time for each VM or group of VMs. The console lists the VMs and the size of the last difference.
3. **Move.** For each VM: stop it, run one last copy, re-home it to the returned pool (the same steps as failover), start it. The outage is the length of the last copy plus the boot. The command refuses to move a VM while its protection is more than the RPO target behind, unless the admin accepts that.
4. **Close.** When the zone's pool holds all its VMs again, the original protection (zone 1 to zone 2) resumes, and the reverse protection ends.

An admin may also choose to **never fail back**. The VMs stay in the partner zone and the returned pool becomes a spare. This costs nothing and has no outage.

### If both versions of a VM changed (partition case)

If a VM kept running in the cut-off zone, its old disk has data that the copy does not have. The failover keeps the old disk as it was, under `/stale/<time>/`, and the status lists it. The admin can look at it with the existing backup browse feature. The driver never merges two versions.

## Consistency of VMs with several disks

Level 1: all disks are on `rfs` shares, which are synchronous. The result is as a power cut would leave it. A VM with several disks on different shares is as consistent as after a power cut, because writes reached the storage in order for each file. The guest's own journal handles the rest. This is the same state a physical server would be in.

Level 2: the existing VM copy takes all disks of a VM at one moment, and the failover picks the newest moment that all disks show. If the guest agent works, the result is file-system consistent. If not, it is crash consistent. The status says which. This is the same method as cross-region replication, so no new code is needed for it.

Plain volumes (not VM disks) in both levels: files are as consistent as the files are. A database that writes several files needs its own method (a hook, or an application-level copy).

## Interaction with other features

- **Instant VM snapshots.** The overlay files of a disk are in the disk's folder, so the copy method takes the folder as it does for cross-region replication. Phase 0 checks that a disk with a chain boots in the partner zone after a Level 2 failover. A snapshot taken in the failed zone and not yet copied is lost with the RPO.
- **Backups.** The snapshot pool for backups must not be in a zone it protects. The Helm check warns if the snapshot pool is in the same zone as the pool it backs up. COS export is independent of zones and is the last-resort source.
- **Cross-region DR.** A cross-region policy copies a pool to another region. After a zone failover, the volumes live in the partner pool, so the cross-region policy must follow them. The policy selects by label, and the first run after a re-home is a compare pass. The `/replica/` folders are not volumes, so cross-region policies skip them.
- **Drain, auto-expand, tiers.** Unchanged. The partner pool counts the copy space as used space, so auto-expand reacts to it.
- **Golden images and CDI.** A CDI import in the failed zone fails and retries in a surviving zone through the normal StorageClass binding. No special handling.

## Console and command line

Console (a new "Zones" page in the existing plugin):

- A row for each zone: state, workers ready, pools, protection status, spare room.
- A row for each protected pool: partner, last good copy, RPO now against target, bytes behind.
- A button "Fail over zone". It opens the plan: VMs, RPO for each, spare room, fence steps. The admin types the zone name to confirm. A progress view follows the `ZoneFailover` steps.
- A "Fail back" button on a failed-over zone, with the VM list and the per-VM move.

Command line, extending the existing failover tool:

```
kubectl failover zone status
kubectl failover zone plan    --zone eu-de-1 [--pool NAME] [--rewind-to TIME]
kubectl failover zone execute --zone eu-de-1 --approve [--accept-unfenced]
kubectl failover zone failback plan|execute --zone eu-de-1 [--vm NS/NAME]
```

## Monitoring and alerts

New metrics (the 31-metric table in `MONITORING.md` grows):

- `zone_state{zone}` (0 healthy, 1 suspect, 2 failed, 3 failed over, 4 recovering)
- `zone_protection_rpo_seconds{pool,partner}` and per VM `zone_protection_vm_rpo_seconds`
- `zone_protection_last_success_timestamp{pool}`
- `zone_headroom_ratio{zone}` for CPU, memory and pool space
- `zone_failover_step_duration_seconds{step}` and `zone_failover_total{result}`
- `zone_fence_state{zone}`

Alerts:

- Zone suspect (warning), zone failed (critical).
- Protection behind target for 2 intervals (warning), for 6 (critical).
- Spare room below what one zone loss needs (warning).
- A partner pool above 85% (warning).
- A VM running with no protection while its pool has protection on (warning).
- A fence step failed (critical).

## Cost

Storage and traffic:

- **Level 1:** `rfs` capacity price, and bandwidth above 800 Mbps costs extra. IBM publishes the price in the console only. Not found in the docs. Verify. No second copy to buy.
- **Level 2:** the partner pool holds a copy of the protected data. VM copies hold only used blocks (the staging copy is sparse), and the restore points add up to about one more disk for each VM for 24 hours. Plan **1.2 to 2 times the protected data** in the partner pool. The partner pool is still `dp2`, so IOPS is paid on it too.
- IBM share replication would be 1 times the full **capacity** of each share plus its IOPS, whatever the data size.
- Traffic: the copies cross zones inside the region. The docs say that data transfer between regions is billed, and do not say it for zones. Verify.

Compute: the real cost is **spare room**. To survive one zone of three, each zone runs at no more than 66% of its workers. That is 50% more workers than the load needs. This is the largest cost, and it is the same for both levels. It is a property of the goal, not of this driver. With two zones the spare room is 100%.

Load on the VMs: the existing copy slows a VM's writes. At the 20 MB/s default the VM kept 93% of its write speed in the lab. Level 1 has no such cost, but writes are slower than on `dp2` (fact 4).

## Risk

- **`rfs` is select availability.** IBM can change it before general availability. Level 1 may not be offered to every customer. The design must work without it, which is why Level 2 exists.
- **`rfs` performance.** A fixed 35,000 IOPS per share and higher write latency may not suit every VM. Phase 0 measures this with the fio benchmark we already have.
- **Fencing is hard to prove.** A partition cannot be simulated exactly. The lab tests cover the controls we own (security group rule, Node delete, taint, lock). A real zone loss may differ.
- **Re-home touches PVs and PVCs.** A bug here can leave a VM without a disk. The re-home keeps the old objects until the new ones are bound, and the plan shows every change first. The release test covers it.
- **Level 2 depends on the VM copy method**, which Red Hat does not support (accepted on 20 Oct). A zone failover inherits that.
- **A surviving zone has less spare room than expected.** The plan checks the room before it starts.

## Testing

### Lab needed

The three current labs are single-zone. A zone loss needs a new lab:

- A ROVS cluster in **eu-de** with workers in **all three zones** (eu-de-1, eu-de-2, eu-de-3). At least 2 workers per zone, so the other zones can take the load. Size the workers for 3 to 4 test VMs per zone.
- A pool in each zone (Level 2), and a regional pool if IBM gives us `rfs` special access. Ask the IBM account team now, because this takes the longest.
- The driver image from the build server, as for the other labs.
- The same test VM set as the release test (Linux with the guest agent, a Windows VM, a VM with two disks, a VM with an instant-snapshot chain).

No test can switch off an IBM zone. We simulate it in two ways, and each test says which it used:

1. **Cut off:** a network ACL on the zone's worker subnets that blocks all traffic to the other zones and to the storage. Workers stay running. This is the partition case and tests fencing.
2. **Gone:** stop all workers of the zone through the VPC API. This tests the clean zone loss.

### Tests

Each is a release test stage that runs unattended, as the other stages do.

- Level 2, gone: write a counter file on a VM and on a plain volume every second, fail over, check that the data on the new VM is no older than the RPO, and that the VM boots. Repeat for the two-disk VM and check that both disks show one moment.
- Level 2, cut off: as above, but the old VM keeps writing. Check that the fence stops it, that the stale disk is kept, and that no disk of the new VM changed after the failover.
- Level 1, gone and cut off: RPO is 0 (the counter shows no gap), the VM restarts in another zone, and the cut-off VM can no longer write.
- Failback of both levels, with the one-VM move and the outage time measured.
- Spare room: a plan against a full surviving zone is refused.
- Failover with a stuck pod on a missing node (the finalizer case).
- A false alarm: the zone is `Suspect` for 3 minutes and then recovers. Nothing is moved.
- Metrics and alerts fire as listed.

Unit tests cover the zone state machine, the plan builder, the re-home steps with a fake client, and the webhook rules.

## Phase 0: checks in the lab, before any code

Do these first. The results can change the plan.

1. **IBM access:** request `rfs` special access for the account and region. Record the answer and the date.
2. **Cross-zone mounting:** mount a `dp2` share from a worker in another zone through the driver. Does it work? How slow? Does the pool need one mount target per zone, or does one serve all (the two docs disagree)?
3. **A zonal share in a dead zone:** with the zone cut off, can a worker in a surviving zone still mount and use the share? (If it can, the "storage-only-up" shortcut in the failure model is real.)
4. **Share replication:** create a replica in another zone. Measure the real interval, the sync time, and whether a sync is one consistent moment (write a counter during a sync). Fail over with `split`. Resolve fact 11. This decides whether IBM replication has any role.
5. **`rfs` with the driver:** create an `rfs` share through the API, with the Go SDK we use. Mount it with `sec=sys` and our mount options. Check the root squash behaviour, `initial_owner`, the export path, a 1 TB file, and the speed with fio. Fail the zone (cut off) and measure the time until I/O works from another zone.
6. **Fencing controls:** does removing a security group rule stop an open NFS connection? Does a zombie client's write fail after its lease ends? Does deleting a Node object restart an `Always` VM in another zone, and how long does it take?
7. **ROKS and ROVS in a zone loss:** how workers are spread across zones, and whether ROKS replaces lost workers by itself. Find the IBM documentation. Add the source to this spec.
8. **PVC re-home:** delete and recreate a PVC and PV under a VM whose node is gone. Does the VM start on the new PV with its name unchanged?
9. **Cost:** read the price of `rfs` and `dp2` in the console for eu-de, and the VPC rule for traffic between zones. Fill the cost section with numbers.
10. **A disk with an instant-snapshot chain** after a Level 2 copy: does it boot in the partner pool?

Phase 0 ends with a short note in this folder with the results. If check 5 fails or `rfs` is not available, Level 1 waits and the rest goes ahead.

## Decisions I made for you

- Two protection levels: a regional pool for RPO 0, and a copied pool for RPO of minutes. I did not pick one, because the regional shares are not generally available and have performance limits.
- For `dp2` there is no way to reach RPO 0 on IBM Cloud. I state the gap: minutes, not zero.
- The copied pool uses our own VM-safe copy, not IBM share replication. IBM replication is slower (15 minutes or more), copies whole shares, and has no VM moment. It stays a phase 0 comparison only.
- Failover is started by an admin. The controller only proposes it. A false alarm that starts a second copy of every VM is worse than a few minutes of waiting.
- Fencing comes first and is required. The command refuses to run without it, unless the admin says in writing the zone is gone.
- The default copy interval is 5 minutes, and the default RPO target is 15 minutes.
- Zones copy in a ring (1 to 2, 2 to 3, 3 to 1). It is simple and uses the existing one-destination policy.
- The copy folders sit on the partner pool's shares under `/replica/`, not as volumes. A pod cannot mount a copy by mistake.
- After a failover, volumes are re-homed in the same cluster by recreating the PV and PVC under the same names. The VM manifest does not change.
- A stale disk from a cut-off zone is kept, never merged or deleted, until the admin removes it.
- Failback is optional and per VM, with a short outage. Staying in the new zone forever is allowed.
- The spare room is part of the plan. A failover plan is refused if the surviving zones cannot hold the load, unless the admin overrides.
- A zone-loss test is simulated (blocked network, stopped workers). We cannot switch off an IBM zone.
- A new multi-zone lab in eu-de is needed. None of the current three can test this.
- The automatic failover is the last phase and off by default.
- I did not use accessor zones as a protection method. They help with load, not with the loss of the zone that holds the share.
